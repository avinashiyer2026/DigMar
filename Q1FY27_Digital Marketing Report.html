What Happened

  • Clicks grew from 8,580 (Apr) to 19,362 (Jun) — up 126%
  • Engagement rate climbed from 40.1% to 66.4%
  • Posting volume stayed flat at 14–17 posts/month over the same period

What It Means for Us

  • The lift is coming entirely from content quality, not quantity
  • LinkedIn is functioning as the credibility channel where GCs, CISOs, and legal ops leaders see LDM as people, not just a vendor line item
  • 66%+ engagement is well outside typical B2B benchmarks (2–5%), confirming "human-centric" is the right creative direction
  • Gap: LinkedIn referral traffic is only 3.1% of website sessions in June — engagement isn't yet fully converting into site visits

What's Next

  • Formalize "real people, real moments" as a standing content pillar rather than an occasional format
  • Test a small paid-boost budget on top organic posts — the organic proof-of-concept is strong
  • Add a clearer link-back path (comments, CTAs) to convert LinkedIn engagement into website sessions

We track 20 keywords in total — that number hasn't changed since February. Within it, our page-1 presence weakened while lower-tier rankings grew, meaning keywords are sliding down the results page rather than climbing up.

What Happened

  • Page-1 rankings fell 33% since February (9 → 6)
  • Page 6–10 rankings doubled over the same window (3 → 6)
  • Total tracked keywords (20) and Domain Authority (27) have been static since at least October 2025

What It Means for Us

  • Top-of-funnel discoverability for buyer-intent search terms softened this quarter, likely linked to the March–April Google core update the agency flagged
  • Flat DA is capping how much on-page optimization alone can achieve — authority is the ceiling
  • Most Search Console clicks are branded queries ("ldm", "ldm global") rather than topic searches — we're being found by people who already know us, not yet by people searching the problems we solve

What's Next

  • Expand beyond the current static 20-keyword set — prioritize service + jurisdiction long-tail terms (e.g., breach notification review by state)
  • Refresh content for the 6 keywords still on page 1 to defend position before they slip further
  • Invest in backlinks/PR to move Domain Authority off 27 — flat DA is the structural constraint here

What Happened

  • Page views up 22% in June, sessions up 6% over the quarter
  • Engagement rate up 13.8% and time on page up 19% over the quarter

What It Means for Us

  • Raw traffic isn't the health metric to chase right now — visit quality is up double digits across the board
  • The smaller pool of visitors we get is doing more once they arrive
  • This mirrors what the April report itself flagged: a decline in traffic alongside better quality traffic
  • Open question: we don't currently have GA4 conversion goals set up to prove this is translating into business outcomes

What's Next

  • Set up GA4 conversion goals (contact form, "get to know us" completions) to connect engagement quality to pipeline
  • Keep optimizing for depth of engagement over raw traffic volume in the short term
  • Revisit the traffic decline once SEO page-1 rankings (Section 2) are addressed — the two are likely linked

Audience Demographics

Where our engaged visitors are coming from — geographically and by acquisition channel — based on June's Google Analytics snapshot (999 active users, 44.4% engagement rate).

Active Users by Country (Top Geographies)

US + India = 65.7% of active users

Traffic Acquisition Channel

What Happened

  • US delivers 46.75% of active users, India 18.92%, UK 4% — followed by a long tail of Bangladesh, Singapore, and China
  • Direct visits (52.4%) and organic search (42.4%) together account for nearly all traffic
  • Social and referral remain minor at 3.3% and 1.6%

What It Means for Us

  • A near-47% US share is a strong fit signal — it matches our AmLaw 50 / Magic Circle target market directly
  • India's 18.9% share likely blends genuine market interest with internal/delivery-center traffic — worth reading with caution
  • The UK's 4% is worth watching given our active healthcare expansion target there
  • Heavy Direct + Organic Search skew confirms most visitors already know to look for us by name — content and backlinks aren't yet driving significant new-visitor discovery

What's Next

  • Segment out internal/India office traffic in future reports to isolate true external market engagement
  • Build UK-specific healthcare content to grow that secondary ABM target intentionally rather than passively
  • Invest in referral/backlink sources to reduce dependence on direct/branded traffic

Assumptions & Data Notes

How source data was interpreted while building this report — flagged here for transparency, not buried in the numbers above.

  • "3 months" = Q2 2026 (April, May, June), matching the three agency reports provided. Where useful, prior months (from the same tables) are referenced for context.
  • "Blogs published in the last 90 days" is read as the April–June window: 12 blog posts (3 + 4 + 5) in total.
  • Data restatement: the June report restates May's "Avg. time on blog page" as 56s, versus 4m 00s originally reported in the May deck. The June (most recent) figure is used here; flagging this so the discrepancy isn't mistaken for an error in this report.
  • GSC "Avg. Position" for April was listed as "15.1%" in the source deck — treated as 15.1 (an unlabeled SERP position, not a percentage), consistent with all other months in that row.
  • Individual blog-level performance (which specific article performed best) is not broken out in the agency's reports — only aggregate monthly totals are provided. Recommend requesting per-URL data next cycle (see Section 4).
  • Demographics and traffic-source figures use the June snapshot (most recent and most complete breakdown provided).
