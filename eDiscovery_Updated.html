What's at stake

The real cost of discovery done poorly

Poor eDiscovery practice doesn't just create operational friction — it creates legal exposure. These risks are well-established in case law and regulatory precedent.

Litigation Risk
Spoliation findings and adverse inference instructions
Failure to preserve potentially relevant ESI — or inability to demonstrate that preservation was adequate — exposes parties to spoliation motions, adverse inference instructions, and case-dispositive sanctions. The duty to preserve attaches before litigation is filed. Your review partner must be able to demonstrate both what was done and why.
Regulatory Exposure
Incomplete productions compound the underlying investigation
Regulators treat production quality as a proxy for compliance posture. A production that is late, over-redacted, or methodologically indefensible doesn't just fail to satisfy the inquiry — it signals broader organizational risk and often extends the investigation.
Cost Overrun
Discovery cost expands with data volume unless controlled from day one
Without early case assessment and a structured review workflow, discovery cost scales proportionally with the data universe — regardless of what percentage of that data is actually relevant. The organizations that control discovery cost do so because they make the right technology and workflow decisions in the first week, not the third month.
